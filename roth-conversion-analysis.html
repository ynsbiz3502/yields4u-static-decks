How the numbers are built

Method and disclosures

  • All figures are projections computed from the assumptions below, not a filed return. The 2026 baseline appears under “Where you stand today” and every number updates when an assumption changes.
  • 2026 federal law as enacted by the One Big Beautiful Bill Act: permanent 10/12/22/24/32/35/37% brackets, $32,200 standard deduction plus $1,650 per spouse over 65, and the $6,000-per-person senior deduction for 2025 through 2028 only. Brackets, deductions and IRMAA thresholds grow with the inflation assumption after 2026.
  • Social Security taxation uses the statutory $32,000 and $44,000 thresholds, which are not indexed.
  • Medicare IRMAA uses the 2026 surcharges for Part B and Part D. Each year's income sets the premium two years later.
  • RMDs begin at 75 for anyone born in 1960 or later (SECURE 2.0) and use the Uniform Lifetime Table on the prior year-end balance. Both IRAs are combined and measured on the older spouse's age.
  • State and local income tax are modeled as a single flat rate on income other than Social Security, after a $2,000-per-senior exemption. This is a simplification — confirm your state’s treatment of retirement income.
  • Both spouses are assumed to live through the projection and file jointly. A surviving spouse files single with narrower brackets, which raises the tax on the same IRA income and makes conversions more valuable than shown here.
  • Returns are applied evenly each year. Real markets vary, and the order of returns affects outcomes.
  • This analysis is for planning discussion. It is not a tax return and does not replace advice on your full situation. Confirm figures with your tax preparer before executing a conversion.
