2What the years actually look like

✓
Your income and your insurance carry the early years. For the first three years of care, your pension, Social Security, and your long-term-care policy cover most of the bill before your savings are touched.
✓
The pressure starts in year four — when the policy runs out. Your long-term-care policy pays for 36 months, then stops. That's the moment the full cost lands on your own resources, and it's the moment a plan matters most.
✓
Even then, you don't run out — and your estate likely survives. In every realistic path, the life insurance is never touched. The legacy you said wasn't a priority ends up protected anyway.

3The move

The plan that turns "you're fine" into "you're handled."

This is what having a plan actually looks like.

Knowing the money lasts is not the same as knowing which dollars to spend. Here's the order we put the pieces in — so no one has to guess under pressure, and so you never pay a dollar of tax you didn't have to.

1
Cash bridges the first 90 days. Your policy pays nothing for three months. We set aside cash so no one sells investments in a panic.
2
The long-term-care policy carries months 4 through 39. $6,000 a month you've already paid for — it does the heavy lifting early, then hands off.
3
Savings are spent in the right tax order. Brokerage and cash first, the IRA next, and the Roth last — so your tax-free money keeps growing as your reserve.
4
The home funds the long stay. When a move becomes permanent, the house becomes the engine that carries a multi-year stay.
5
Life insurance is the last line — and usually stays untouched. Which is how you end up leaving something behind without ever planning to.
